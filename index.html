The problem

Non-Revenue Water is water treated, pumped, and never paid for.

Across Bangladesh, utilities pump clean water into networks where it leaks out through broken pipes, illegal connections and faulty meters — unbilled, untraced, and unpaid.

The solution

Three layers of detection, one ranked queue of field actions.

AquaX starts with the data utilities already have today, then grows into the network and the sky.

LIVE · WORKING MVP

Layer 1 — Billing anomaly detection

Risk-scores every connection from its billing and consumption history: sudden unexplained drops, dormant meters, abnormal patterns. Each flag ships with a plain-language reason and a recommended field action.

YEAR 2

Layer 2 — DMA flow & pressure anomaly

Listens to district-metered-area flow and pressure streams. Night-flow analysis and pressure-drop signatures localise leaks to a zone before a customer ever complains.

YEAR 2–3

Layer 3 — Sentinel-1 SAR leak mapping

Free radar imagery from ESA's Copernicus Sentinel-1 detects soil-moisture anomalies along buried mains — satellite-scale leak hunting with zero proprietary contract.

All three feed one dashboard that ranks by litres and revenue recoverable.

Why AquaX is different

Built for the gap nobody else is filling.

  • The flood-forecasting field is crowded; water-loss intelligence is not. Every AI-for-water team in the region is predicting floods. Almost nobody is helping utilities stop losing the water they already treat.Team analysis of GP FutureMakers problem spaces
  • No Bangladeshi water utility runs AI-based predictive loss detection today. Loss control is still complaint-driven and manual.Source: UNB News
  • AquaX sits on infrastructure that already exists. Dhaka WASA has rolled out district metering in 62 of its 164 zones — the data plumbing for Layer 2 is being laid right now.Source: UNB News
  • Free, open satellite data instead of proprietary contracts. The satellite-leak-detection approach (proven commercially by ASTERRA-class services worldwide) is replicated on Copernicus Sentinel-1 — no six-figure licence.Source: ASTERRA via Kurrant News, 2025
